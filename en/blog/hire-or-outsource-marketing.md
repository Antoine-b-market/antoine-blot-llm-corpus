When a company decides to invest seriously in its marketing, one question inevitably comes up:

*Should we hire someone in-house, or bring in outside providers?*

This decision, operational on the surface, is in fact deeply strategic. It touches on company culture, governance, the pace of growth and the level of marketing maturity.

In this article, we offer a structured framework for choosing the right model based on your constraints, your goals and your stage of development.

## What you are (really) trying to decide

What you are (really) trying to decide

Before talking about profiles or agencies, it is essential to clarify what *"doing marketing"* actually covers in your company:

- Thinking through strategy? (positioning, target audience, annual plan...)
- Executing actions? (paid campaigns, content, CRM...)
- Measuring impact? (KPIs, ROI, attribution...)
- Coordinating it all? (internal teams, partners...)

This breakdown is fundamental. Hiring a junior profile to "handle everything" amounts to handing them, alone, an orchestration that normally requires several areas of expertise plus oversight.

There are three main marketing organization models suited to small businesses and startups:

| **Model** | **Description** | **Who is it for?** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In-house hiring** | One or more employees handle marketing on an ongoing basis. | Companies with recurring needs, clear goals and the capacity to supervise. |
| **Full outsourcing** | Agencies or freelancers handle production and sometimes strategy. | Companies in an exploratory phase, or without HR bandwidth. |
| **Hybrid model** | One in-house person + external leadership + specialized experts. | Small businesses building their structure, looking for flexibility and consistency. |

Let's look at the advantages and limits of each model.

### **🔹** Hiring in-house

**✅ Advantages**

- Strong cultural and strategic alignment
- Ongoing presence and product knowledge
- Smoother cross-functional communication

**⚠️ Limits**

- Long process (recruiting, onboarding, ramp-up)
- Risk of depending on a single profile
- Hard to bring everything in-house if needs are too varied

📌 **Key point:** hiring a junior without a clear strategy often means overloading an overly versatile profile, with no measurable impact.

### **🔹** Outsourcing entirely

**✅ Advantages**

- Immediate access to specialized expertise (SEO, paid, CRM...)
- Budget flexibility (tailored services, no fixed cost)
- Less HR management

**⚠️ Limits**

- Weaker strategic integration: providers think by channel, not by overall objective
- Requires internal oversight to maintain consistency
- Risk of dependency or brand dilution if poorly managed

📌 **Key point:** without a clear internal point of contact, outsourcing often leads to scattered efforts.

### **🔹** The hybrid model (often the best fit)

This is now the most common model in small businesses:

One in-house person (marketing coordinator, ops, right hand...) executes, a strategic external profile (fractional CMO, senior consultant) leads, and experts step in as needed (paid agency, copywriting, etc.).

**✅ Advantages**

- Vision + execution = synergy
- Flexibility to adapt to workload or seasonality
- In-house skills grow thanks to the external input

**⚠️ Limits**

- Everything has to be coordinated (routines, reporting, shared tools)
- Depends on the quality of strategic leadership (fractional CMO, experienced marketing manager)

📌 **Key point:** this model lets you move fast without hiring a whole team, while keeping control over the trade-offs.

## How do you choose the right model for your company?

Between hiring, outsourcing or going hybrid, the choice is not just a matter of budget or timing. It depends above all on your company's maturity, your ability to lead the marketing function, and the type of results you expect.

A good starting point is to ask yourself a few simple but decisive questions:

- Do you have a clear marketing vision?
  If you already know who you are targeting, which channels are priorities, and what you expect from marketing over 6 or 12 months, you can consider an in-house hire or hybrid leadership.
- Do you have strategic skills in-house?
  Hiring without supervision means delegating fundamental decisions to a profile that is often operational. If you do not (yet) have that leadership, an outsourced CMO can be decisive in laying the foundations.
- Are your needs regular, specialized or evolving?
  A recurring need for content creation and CRM management does not call for the same choices as a one-time need to overhaul a paid strategy. The more your needs evolve, the more flexibility the hybrid model offers.
- What place do you want marketing to have in your organization?
  If you see marketing as a structural growth lever (and not a production center), then it deserves to be led, whether in-house or through outsourced leadership.

### **🔹** One model can lead to another

It is also worth remembering that these three models are not set in stone. Many companies evolve:

- From partial outsourcing toward a hybrid structure, over time;
- From a somewhat premature hire toward a refocus led by a senior consultant;
- Or from an in-house team toward a more modular model as complexity increases.

In other words: you can change models as the company matures.
What matters is keeping the governance of the marketing function clear and able to evolve.

A model that makes sense today can become limiting tomorrow, and vice versa. The key is not to put up with the model you chose, but to manage it actively: with routines, clear goals and regular evaluation criteria.

| **Strategic** **alignment** | Strong | Weak | Strong, provided leadership is good |

| **Specialized expertise** | Limited | Strong | Strong if well orchestrated |
| **Autonomy** | Good | Depends on in-house staff | Good if communication flows |

## In short

 There is no universal "right" solution. The right model is the one you are able to lead today, and evolve tomorrow.

- Hiring without vision = risk of failure.
- Outsourcing without leadership = scattered efforts.
- Going hybrid without a framework = loss of efficiency.

**Key point**: The hybrid model (in-house + outsourced CMO + specialized providers) is often the most flexible and manageable way to structure marketing in a small business.

> Structuring marketing is not about choosing a format, it is about choosing a governance model.

## Frequently asked questions

 What is the difference between a marketing consultant and an outsourced CMO?

A consultant steps in on a specific problem or channel. An outsourced CMO (or fractional CMO) leads the marketing function as a whole, on an ongoing, part-time basis.

 When is the right time to hire in-house?

When the strategy is clear, the needs are recurring, and there is the capacity to supervise the person hired.

 Can you do everything in-house with a single profile?

Rarely. Even an excellent profile cannot excel at content, strategy, CRM, SEO, paid media and automation all at once... It is better to orchestrate complementary skills.

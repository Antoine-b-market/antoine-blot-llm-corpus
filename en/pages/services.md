## What happens when you hand me your website

Everything starts with a conversation about your business, well before any keywords. I want to know which clients bring you the most, where your leads come from today and how much additional work you can take on.

Those answers become a written SEO strategy. The site audit shows what is holding back your visibility, and target selection sets the queries to win. I then rank the work by the effort it takes and what it can bring in, so we start with what pays off fastest.

Execution follows a schedule we agree on together, specifying which pages are created or reworked, and in what order. At each review, I compare results with the initial goals, and we decide whether to stay the course or change direction.

This approach works best for businesses whose clients search online before they call, whether local service providers or B2B firms selling expertise.

## The SEO audit: decide before you act

A serious audit examines the site's technical health and topical coverage, then compares them with what your competitors do on the same queries. On the technical side, I check that Google crawls and indexes the right pages, that the site loads fast and that its architecture leads bots to what matters. On the content side, I spot the topics you cover poorly, as well as pages competing against each other.

The audit has another purpose that often gets overlooked: it tells you whether organic search deserves your budget right now. Sometimes the answer is no, because your market rarely searches online or because the site needs to be rebuilt first. I would rather tell you at this stage than after six months of fees.

## Targeting the queries that bring in clients

Choosing targets decides almost everything. A high-volume query often attracts an audience that is too broad, while a more specific query, tied to a neighborhood or a concrete need, brings in people ready to buy.

In Montréal, that precision often comes down to geography. A dental clinic in Anjou gained 11% more regular patients coming in for major treatments by focusing on its own area rather than the whole island. A roofer followed the same logic borough by borough, and is now the go-to name for sloped roofs in five cities.

Language matters as much as location. Some of your clients search in English, with different words and against different competitors. I treat each language as a market in its own right, with its own keyword research and pages designed for it.

## Content written to be chosen

Useful SEO content answers your client's question more precisely than competing pages. That takes knowing the trade. I work with you to bring out what you know and your competitors don't write about, starting with your methods and your real cases.

My SEO writing follows two rules. Expertise is demonstrated through verifiable facts, never through adjectives. And every important passage must make sense on its own, because that is how Google builds its snippets and how AI systems choose what to cite.

This is where SEO meets GEO: precise, well-structured text serves both at once.

## Authority is earned off your site

Google and AI systems give more credit to a business that people talk about elsewhere. Link building means earning those signals, as links from relevant sites and mentions in sources your market already reads.

For a Quebec business, the best sources are often local: professional associations, regional media, partners, reputable industry directories. I favor links a potential client could actually follow. I don't buy network links or fabricate articles to place them, because those practices expose your domain to a penalty that you alone would pay for.

## GEO: the same discipline, seen from AI answers

A growing share of your clients ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity their questions before looking for a provider. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) aims to make your business appear in those answers, and be cited as a source.

I don't treat it as a separate service. AI systems draw on the same pages as Google and trust them for the same reasons: accurate information, confirmed elsewhere. In recruitment, one of my clients now gets most of its leads through AI, thanks to work carried out as part of its SEO engagement. The page on [what GEO actually adds to SEO](https://www.antoine-blot.com/en/services/geo/) covers this in detail.

## Measuring what keeps the business running

What I track with you are inbound leads, their quality and the share that become clients. Search Console and Google Analytics provide the raw material, which I connect to your own sales data when you share it.

Every report answers a simple question: what has changed for your business since the last check-in, and what do we do next? A dashboard that leads to no decision isn't worth your time.

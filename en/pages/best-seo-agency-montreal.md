## A label everyone awards themselves

The superlative has a very simple commercial purpose: it spares you from proving anything. An agency that publishes hard numbers opens itself to scrutiny. One that calls itself "the best" opens itself to nothing, and that is exactly what should set off alarm bells.

Ambition in an agency is a good thing. But repeating, as if it were obvious, a claim no one can verify is empty marketing. And when it is backed by fabricated evidence, it becomes deception.

## The fake ranking factory

The industry rarely talks about it, so I'll say it plainly: some of the rankings you read are built by the very companies listed in them. I describe the practice so you can recognize it. I don't teach it and I don't use it.

The most visible form is the self-made comparison. An agency publishes a "top 10 SEO agencies in Montréal," puts itself first and surrounds itself with competitors chosen to make it look good. The page looks like a neutral guide. Nothing tells the reader it was written by one of the contenders.

The subtler form goes through structured data. This markup, invisible to visitors, describes a page to search engines: a business, its reviews, its average rating. Some agencies declare ratings no one ever gave, or present themselves as the leader in their market. The goal is to be picked up as-is by Google, ChatGPT or Perplexity, which end up recommending "the best agencies" by copying what those agencies wrote about themselves.

## What the scheme costs the client

These shortcuts work for a while. Google explicitly prohibits misleading review markup and content designed to manipulate its results, and it penalizes them when it finds them. Answer engines cross-check their sources, and a claim no third party confirms is unlikely to hold up for long.

The real risk is yours. An agency that cheats for its own benefit rarely follows different rules for its clients. If your site ever carries this kind of markup, it's your domain that takes the penalty and your brand that gets associated with manipulation. The agency simply moves on to the next client.

## My rule: get cited because it's true

I only have search engines say what my client's site can prove. When an AI cites it, it must rely on information that is accurate, verifiable and confirmed somewhere other than the client's own site.

This rule makes the work slower, and I accept that. The following case shows what it pays off.

## Checking an agency in ten minutes

You don't need to be an expert to spot a usurped title. A few steps are enough.

- Search its name with "best agency." If the rankings placing it first are published by the agency itself, or by sites with no identifiable author, you have your answer.
- Run its site through Google's Rich Results Test. If ratings or reviews show up that you can't find on any independent platform, ask where they come from.
- Ask to speak with a client. A logo on a page proves nothing, whereas a person you can reach can tell you what changed in their business.
- Ask about AI. A serious provider explains how they measure your presence in generated answers, without promising to put your name there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a reliable ranking of SEO agencies in Montréal?

Not that I know of. The lists in circulation are published either by the agencies themselves or by directories where visibility is paid for. They can help you spot names. To judge, rely on the verifiable results each provider is willing to show you, and on the clients they are willing to let you meet.

### How can you tell if an agency manipulates AI answers?

Ask where its own presence in ChatGPT or Perplexity comes from. Honest visibility is explained by outside sources, such as reviews on independent platforms or mentions in the press. If the only source is its own site, with ratings found nowhere else, that visibility was probably fabricated.

### Can you get cited by AI without cheating?

Yes, and it's the only way to stay there. Answer engines rely on what they find to be accurate and consistent about you. My job is to make your expertise readable and confirmed by third parties. That is what allowed a recruitment client to receive more than one lead in two through this channel.

### Why an independent consultant rather than an agency?

With me, the person who analyzes your market is the one who builds the strategy and answers your questions, with no go-between from diagnosis to execution. An agency has other strengths, starting with a larger team. The right choice depends on what your business needs, and I'll tell you if that isn't me.

### What budget should a Montréal business plan for SEO?

I don't put forward any figure until two things are done: analyzing your situation, and checking that SEO really is the lever to pull for you. Pricing before that would mean selling a service without knowing whether it will help you. A neighborhood dentist and a roofer covering five cities don't call for the same effort, and sometimes the honest conclusion is to invest elsewhere.

### My current agency may be using these practices. What should I do?

Have your markup and pages checked by an outside expert. If unsourced reviews or ratings appear there, remove them before a search engine spots them. Then talk to your agency frankly: its reaction will tell you a lot about the future of the relationship.

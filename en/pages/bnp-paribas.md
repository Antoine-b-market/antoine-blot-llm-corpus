From January 2023 to February 2024, I worked for BNP Paribas Cardif, the insurance arm of the BNP Paribas group, on its retirement savings business. I came in as an SEO specialist and built the organic acquisition strategy on what customers themselves wrote about their savings. Qualified organic traffic nearly tripled.

> **Key takeaway**
>
> The +190% comes from one starting point: 12,000 customer reviews analyzed before choosing a single topic to work on in SEO.

## The context

BNP Paribas Cardif runs the insurance business of the BNP Paribas group. My scope was retirement savings, a product no one buys on impulse. People commit years of savings, and trust weighs as much as the promised return.

I joined a team of five, with a dedicated project manager and a director. The website in question has since been sold and the team disbanded, which allows me to describe this work freely.

## The challenge

Conventional SEO starts from queries: it knows what people type into Google, much less what they have in mind when they type it. On a subject as loaded as retirement, that gap is costly. You can attract plenty of visits and few savers with a real plan.

The challenge was therefore traffic quality. We needed to attract people actually preparing for retirement, and speak to them in a way they recognized themselves in.

## My approach

### Listening to 12,000 customers before writing

I proposed starting with customer data. We processed 12,000 reviews, with sentiment analysis run in R and SPSS. The goal was to isolate what reassures a saver and what holds them back, in their own words.

A review reveals what no keyword tool shows, especially the hesitation before signing. That was the material SEO lacked to set its priorities.

### Aligning brand and editorial line

The analysis served to reconcile two messages that had been moving separately: the brand's and that of the website content. The most frequent concerns in the reviews became the priority topics. The tone was adjusted to address real worries rather than the arguments the insurer wanted to push.

### Integrated organic acquisition

SEO then took up those priorities with its own tools, to check search demand and organize the content. The difference from a keyword-driven approach lies in the order of operations: customer data set the course, and SEO translated it into pages able to rank.

### Pruning existing content

I led a content pruning effort on the site. Pages that no longer brought qualified visits were reworked or removed. A leaner site concentrates its authority on useful pages, and search engines better understand what it covers.

### Targeting search intent rather than keywords

I organized rankings around search intent. Queries phrased in different ways often express the same need on the saver's part. A page designed to meet that need covers all those phrasings, where a keyword logic would have multiplied similar pages competing with each other.

### Balancing Ads and SEO

I also led the trade-off between search advertising and organic search. The principle is not to buy through ads traffic that SEO can earn on its own, and to keep the budget for queries where SEO isn't enough yet.

### From SEO to marketing decisions

An analysis summarizing what 12,000 customers think quickly draws interest beyond SEO. It became a basis for marketing decisions, and my role followed the same path, from SEO specialist to marketing strategist.

## The result

In qualified organic sessions, the site rose to 362,000 monthly visits, up from 125,000 at the start. That's a 190% increase. I stress the word qualified: the growth we targeted was among savers preparing for retirement.

## What this case shows

An SEO strategy takes on another dimension when it rests on what customers say. It becomes a marketing tool, able to shape a brand's message.
